# Functional Movement Systems / Gray Cook

## Who / what

Gray Cook and Lee Burton developed the Functional Movement Screen (FMS) and related systems (including SFMA for people in pain). Core thesis: observe and improve fundamental movement patterns before loading performance qualities.

## Core ideas to keep

- Patterns before parts.
- Mobility before stability before skill/strength loading.
- Screen to establish a baseline; correct the limiting pattern; then develop.
- Correctives should challenge the edge of ability with rich sensory feedback — not just rehearse the sport skill poorly.
- Asymmetry and dysfunction are training inputs, not afterthoughts.
- Foundational “big rocks” often emphasized later by Cook: deadlift/hinge variations, Turkish get-up, farmer’s carry, chop and lift.

## FMS seven screens (reference)

1. Deep Squat
2. Hurdle Step
3. Inline Lunge
4. Shoulder Mobility
5. Active Straight-Leg Raise
6. Trunk Stability Push-Up
7. Rotary Stability

Primitive patterns (mobility/stability building blocks) generally prioritized before the more “functional” loaded patterns in corrective algorithms.

## Product extraction

- Encode the **hierarchy**: if mobility is limited, do not jump to heavy bilateral squats.
- Offer a lightweight self-check questionnaire or simple movement checklist inspired by the philosophy — without claiming official FMS certification or scoring IP.
- Tag exercises as mobility, stability, pattern-retrain, or load.
- SFMA/pain path: if user reports pain, soft-gate to medical advice rather than self-correctives alone.

## IP / product caution

Do not ship branded FMS scoring as a feature without proper licensing. Principles and hierarchy are the durable product asset.
